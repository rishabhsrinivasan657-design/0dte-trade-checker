How each number is worked out

Breakeven

Strike + premium for a call, strike − premium for a put. At 4:00 PM the option is worth exactly how far SPY is past the strike, so you need SPY past the breakeven just to get your money back.

Expected move

From the at-the-money straddle (call + put at the strike nearest SPY). A straddle pays off on a move in either direction, so its price is the market's estimate of how big today's move will be, not which way.

Chance of finishing in the money / of profit

Black-Scholes N(d₂), using this option's own implied volatility (solved from its mid price). "Chance of profit" is the same calculation measured at the breakeven. These are the market's risk-neutral odds, not a guarantee.

Time decay

Theta converted from per-year to per-hour. Because decay speeds up near the close, we also re-price the option one hour later (or at the close) with SPY unchanged.

History check & verdict

How often SPY actually moved past the strike in the same window. The intraday version uses the last ~60 days of 5-minute bars from this time of day to the close; the daily version uses ~3 years of open-to-close moves, scaled to the time left with the square-root-of-time rule. If history beat the market's odds by more than 5 points, it's Cheap; if it fell short by more than 5, Expensive.

Limitations
  • Free option quotes are indicative (or ~15 min delayed from Yahoo), not the official OPRA feed.
  • SPY options are American-style; Black-Scholes is European. At 0DTE the difference is tiny.
  • Black-Scholes assumes one constant volatility; the real market has a volatility smile.
  • History doesn't know about today's news (Fed, CPI, earnings). Past ≠ future.
  • Options usually price above history on purpose (the volatility risk premium), so "Expensive" is common.

Educational tool, not financial advice. Quotes may be delayed or indicative. 0DTE options can lose 100% of their value in hours.
