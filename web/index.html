    The rating

    An ML model trained on 80,000+ past 0DTE trades scores this exact option (distance from SPY, time left, implied vol, the market's odds, recent history, the ML move forecast, VIX, price). Bottom 20% of scores → Avoid, next 20% → Poor odds, the rest → Typical. The Research page shows how each group actually did on months the model never saw.

    Breakeven

    Strike + premium for a call, strike − premium for a put. At 4:00 PM the option is worth exactly how far SPY is past the strike, so you need SPY past the breakeven just to get your money back.

    Expected move

    From the at-the-money straddle (call + put at the strike nearest SPY). A straddle pays off on a move in either direction, so its price is the market's estimate of how big today's move will be, not which way.

    Realistic chance of profit

    Black-Scholes N(d₂) with this strike's implied volatility, measured at your breakeven, then corrected by how often the market's odds actually came true in 2024–2026 (it overstates long shots).

    Time decay

    Theta converted from per-year to per-hour. Because decay speeds up near the close, we also re-price the option one hour later (or at the close) with SPY unchanged.

    History check

    How often SPY actually moved past the strike in the same window: the last ~60 days of 5-minute bars, and ~3 years of daily moves scaled to the time left. A weak signal on its own, so it's one input to the rating.

    Limitations
    • Free option quotes are indicative (or ~15 min delayed from Yahoo), not the official OPRA feed.
    • SPY options are American-style; Black-Scholes is European. At 0DTE the difference is tiny.
    • Black-Scholes assumes one volatility; the real market has a volatility smile.
    • History can't see today's news (Fed, CPI). Past results don't predict the future.

    Educational tool, not financial advice. Quotes may be delayed or indicative. 0DTE options can lose 100% of their value in hours.
